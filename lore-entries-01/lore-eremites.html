Eremites

The Eremites are a broad name for Sumeru’s desert mercenaries and tribes. Their lineages, customs, loyalties, and contracts differ, as the recent record of Nefer’s Shimti kin makes clear.

One name for many groups

The Adventurer's Handbook describes the Eremites as loosely organized mercenary corps and explains that outsiders use the name as an umbrella for the desert tribes. It names groups such as the Farrokhzadan and the Tanit, which have distinct leaders and histories.

Eremite groups work across Sumeru and take contracts in other nations. The shared label does not establish a single command structure or a common political program.

Several ancestral lineages

The game connects Eremites to older desert peoples and to traditions preserved through stories, music, and combat arts. These links do not make every modern group a direct continuation of one kingdom or one religion.

Version 7.1's Dance of Death: Serpent and Scorpion adds a specific lineage record: a letter from Shimti chief Sinuhe says his people traced their ancestry to Tulaytullah, while the Iab and Unut traced theirs to the Thamudians. The letter also describes the Shimti's worship of the Lord of Flowers and their earlier relations with neighboring tribes.

Mercenary work and local leadership

Eremite groups take different jobs, from caravan and settlement security to private expeditions and trade. Some maintain partnerships with the Akademiya or Adventurers' Guild; others operate independently or pursue their own aims.

Dehya's story quest and the Sumeru Archon Quest show internal disputes among named factions, including Deshret's Relics, the Blazing Beasts, and Rahman's group. These episodes describe specific organizations and do not establish one Eremite council or chain of command.

Spirits in some weapons

Some Eremite enemy descriptions explain their elemental attacks through an ominous spirit sealed in a weapon and awakened in battle. The account belongs to those weapon-bearing fighters; it does not say that every Eremite has an innate elemental power.

The game has not identified every spirit's origin or established that all of them belong to the same category. Links to other desert spirits remain questions for comparison with each source.

The desert and the Akademiya

The Sumeru Archon Quest shows that some desert groups resented the Akademiya's neglect of their communities and distrusted the rainforest's scholars. Rahman's faction begins from that grievance and eventually helps the Traveler free Nahida.

In the post-quest A Toast to Victory, Rahman and Setaria discuss bringing education to the desert. This records a change in their cooperation after the sages' rule; it is not evidence that every Eremite group shares Rahman's beliefs or plans.

Tanit, Jeht, and tribal conflict

Golden Slumber and The Dirge of Bilqis follow Jeht, her father Jebrael, and the Tanit tribe. Their story moves from shared expeditions to a struggle over Babel's leadership and Jeht's refusal to harm the Traveler on the tribe's orders.

The quest arc presents one community's loyalties, rivalries, and collapse. Dance of Death: Serpent and Scorpion later gives the Shimti, Iab, and Unut their own history of resource scarcity, violence, and Nefer's revenge, showing how sharply one desert tribe's story can differ from another's.

What the records establish

The in-game records establish a broad desert identity containing separate mercenary groups and tribes, several ancestral lineages, and distinct arrangements with the Akademiya, clients, and other factions. Some traditions and loyalties overlap, while many belong to particular communities or individuals.

No single origin account, leader, or belief system covers all Eremites. The histories of less-documented groups and the long-term shape of their relationships with Sumeru's institutions remain open as new quests add named people and lineages.
