Guizhong

Guizhong, also known as Haagentus, was the God of Dust, an inventor, and a leader remembered in Liyue’s fragmented accounts of the Guili Assembly.

Haagentus, the God of Dust

In the Lantern Rite story A Single Harmony for an Irreplaceable Soul, Cloud Retainer names Guizhong as Haagentus, the God of Dust. She remembers her as outgoing, fond of gatherings, and eager to make inventions. Guizhong’s identity and personality in this scene come through the recollection of an old friend, rather than a surviving account written by Guizhong herself.

The same recollection places her home and social gatherings in the once-prosperous Guili Plains. Cloud Retainer describes Guizhong as the person who kept a table of proud, argumentative adepti talking, laughing, and eating together. Madame Ping, formerly Streetward Rambler, later explains that Guizhong believed human beings could grow through wisdom and effort and should not be underestimated.

The Guili Assembly and its commandments

Stone Tablet Compilations: Vol. I is presented as historian Zichang’s compilation and translation of fragmented ancient tablets. Its account calls Guizhong an ally of the Lord of Rock, says she brought her people north of Mt. Tianheng and taught them agriculture, and connects her name for the Plains of the Returning and Departing to the place later called Guili Plains. The same book describes war between the gods ravaging the plains, Guizhong dying in that conflict, and Rex Lapis leading the people south.

In Treasure Lost, Treasure Found, Soraya reconstructs Guizhong’s Four Commandments as maxims about wisdom, virtue, strength, and unity. Soraya is a scholar interpreting recovered inscriptions, so her proposed history is presented as a reading of evidence. The book, the quest inscriptions, and Cloud Retainer’s much later memories do not function as a single uninterrupted chronicle. They agree that Guizhong’s community was lost in war, but do not provide a complete account of its founding or every stage of its history.

The inventor and the things she made

The records give Guizhong several distinct inventions. Stone Tablet Compilations says she set up the Guizhong Ballista to guard the area near Mt. Tianheng. In the Lantern Rite recollection, Cloud Retainer recalls arguing with her about mechanical principles and being forced to concede that Guizhong’s Obscuro Vulpes Mechanism was the better creation. Cloud Retainer also says she later made finishing adjustments to Guizhong’s ballistic device in her friend’s memory.

The Cleansing Bell is another of Guizhong’s works. Cloud Retainer says it could compose and perform music. Guizhong and Streetward Rambler disagreed about whether machinery could produce music with feeling; Rex Lapis took the bell and designated it for ceremonial use. After Guizhong’s death, Streetward Rambler asked Rex Lapis to keep the bell safe, then used it to play the tune Guizhong had composed. These details come from Cloud Retainer’s account, which adds context to Madame Ping’s earlier version of the bell’s history.

Guizhong and Morax

The Memory of Dust weapon description remembers Guizhong presenting Morax with a stone dumbbell as a pledge and a challenge. Its narration also says that they made no formal contract and describes them as two people who walked the same path for their own reasons. Guizhong’s words imagine building a great city through her wisdom and Morax’s strength. Together with the book and quest accounts, this establishes an important alliance and shared purpose without specifying a romantic relationship.

The item text says Morax never worked out the dumbbell’s mechanism or learned what Guizhong intended to say after challenging him to unlock it. Its unresolved ending is part of the record; the game does not reveal the device’s complete secret.

Her death and an unfinished legacy

Cloud Retainer says that war between the gods reached the Guili Plains and Guizhong was overpowered by an enemy and killed in battle. Stone Tablet Compilations also places her death in the war that destroyed the plains. Neither source names her opponent or gives a detailed account of the battle, and the surviving records do not establish a precise date for her death.

Guizhong’s unfinished manuscripts remained with Cloud Retainer, while her commandments, bell, weapon, and mechanical designs continued to matter to the people who survived her. These remnants reveal how friends and later historians remembered her, but do not settle every question about her origin, private life, or the full chronology of the Guili Assembly. Read each as a separate piece of testimony rather than joining gaps with speculation.
