Onibudou

Junkichi’s serialized Inazuman novel follows oni warrior Yutaka Riko and her cat Onigiri; the surviving in-game text includes excerpts from the Arayama City and Ryuutetsu Cage arcs.

A serialized novel, not a historical chronicle

Junkichi writes Onibudou for Yae Publishing House. The in-game record is presented as a novel, and its fantastical plot should not be read as proof of how the oni lived or what happened to them in Teyvat’s history.

The item preserves four chapter excerpts: chapters 1 and 15 of the Arayama City Arc, and chapters 1 and 14 of the Ryuutetsu Cage Arc. They are separated glimpses rather than a complete account of either arc.

Yutaka Riko and the Arayama City Arc

The story opens with Yutaka Riko reliving a nightmare about the destruction of the oni race. She seeks Kataba Shintetsu, whom she believes betrayed the oni, and travels with Onigiri, a cat carrying a magatama.

The excerpts show Riko fighting soldiers and learning special techniques from spirits. Onigiri’s spirit-sight helps her find spirits and later detect a safe route. These details belong to the novel’s invented adventure.

Ryuutetsu Cage and Riko’s choice

Ryuutetsu Cage is a chasm filled with dangerous iron sand. Riko learns to escape with Onigiri’s help, then encounters nine stranded people and their cat. Although the adults distrust her after noticing her oni heritage, she still commits to getting them out safely.

The scene centers the novel’s conflict between prejudice and individual action: Riko is judged by her ancestry, but chooses to help people who fear her. It does not establish that the fictional hostility or events happened in actual Inazuman history.

Junkichi’s writing is part of the frame

Commissions about Junkichi and editor Shigeru treat Onibudou as an ongoing publication. Readers react to the pacing, and Shigeru objects when Junkichi gives characters unexplained new powers. This is commentary on the author’s serial-writing decisions, separate from the plot excerpts themselves.

For the distinction between the fiction and the publishing frame, read the novel alongside the commissions Is This Novel Amazing? and This Novel Seems... Problematic?.
