Primordial One

Before Sun and Moon describes the Primordial One’s war against the Seven Sovereigns and the creation of four Shades. Fontaine’s Archon Quest later connects the Primordial One with the Heavenly Principles; the book presents the name Phanes as a possibility, not a settled identity.

An ancient power described in a book

The Primordial One appears in Before Sun and Moon, an account preserved in Enkanomiya. The text describes a being who fought the Seven Sovereigns, created four Shades, and shaped the world in which humans live.

The book is one of the most important written sources for Teyvat’s earliest history. Its author lived long after some of the events described, so the source’s point of view and knowledge limits matter.

The name Phanes

The book says the Primordial One may have been Phanes. The word ‘may’ is important: it marks an identification proposed by the narrator, not an absolute statement from an all-knowing source.

Later Fontaine story connects the Primordial One with the Heavenly Principles. This strengthens the relationship between those names, but it does not make every detail in the Enkanomiyan book equally certain.

The war with the Sovereigns

Before Sun and Moon describes a conflict between the Primordial One and the Seven Sovereigns, ancient dragon rulers. This is part of the foundation for the game’s history of elemental authority and the world’s present order.

Recent lore about Nibelung and the Three Moons has added context to this period. The safest approach is to keep direct quest statements, the book’s account, and fan reconstruction in separate layers.

What remains uncertain

The game has not shown every part of the Primordial One’s identity, arrival, or plan. Its current connection to the Heavenly Principles is important, but it does not answer every question about the being’s motives.

When a source uses a tentative phrase or a symbolic image, keep that uncertainty in the explanation instead of turning it into a definite biography.
