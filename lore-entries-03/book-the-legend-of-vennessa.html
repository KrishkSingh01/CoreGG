The Legend of Vennessa

This two-volume Mondstadt ballad remembers Vennessa’s captivity, her revolt against the Aristocrats, and Barbatos’s support, while ending before her later life and the Knights’ institutional history.

A Song from Mondstadt’s Reconstruction

The Legend of Vennessa is a two-volume ballad described in both volumes as a popular song since Mondstadt’s reconstruction. Its celebratory voice and later date matter: it remembers the city’s liberation through song rather than presenting itself as a contemporary record.

The opening toasts freedom, Barbatos, and Vennessa, then says the Anemo Archon’s gift was “not freedom, but just defiance.” The song’s central emphasis is the people’s revolt and Vennessa’s part in it.

Volume I: The Aristocrats and Vennessa’s Captivity

The first volume portrays Mondstadt under the “royals” or Aristocrats as a dungeon where wealth and festivities belong to the privileged, while ordinary people are chained and used in slavery games.

It introduces Vennessa as a fiery-haired woman born free but held in a cell, who continues to pray for her people and for freedom. The song says she came from the southern plains but does not identify a modern region, so her birthplace should remain unspecified here.

Volume II: Barbatos Supports the Revolt

The second volume says Barbatos answers Vennessa’s devotion, comes to her, and asks only to weave her name into a song in return for her friendship. It then tells of Vennessa defeating the drake to which slaves were sacrificed.

The volume describes the winds joining the revolt against the tyrant’s tower. In the song’s closing exchange, Barbatos credits Vennessa with beginning the liberation and says his own songs praise her freedom. The poem therefore remembers the Archon as an ally, while placing Vennessa’s action at the center.

Where the Ballad Ends

The collection ends with Vennessa standing free after the Aristocrats’ overthrow. It does not narrate her later ascension, identify her as the Falcon of the West, or give a full institutional history of the Knights of Favonius.

Those later parts of Vennessa’s story come from other game material. The ballad itself does not name the Lawrence Clan; it uses the broad terms “royals” and “Aristocrats,” so those details should not be folded into the song’s own account.
