The Linguistic Architecture of Teyvat

Throughout the ruins, cities, signposts, and ancient domains of Teyvat, players encounter a fascinating variety of written scripts. Far from being random decorative scribbles, these writing systems were meticulously engineered by the developers as genuine, decipherable substitution ciphers and stylized phonetic alphabets that encode real messages, historical lore, and hidden jokes.

Scholars of the Haravatat Darshan in Sumeru and real-world linguists in the community have cracked these alphabets, allowing Travelers to read shop signs, tavern notices, divine murals, and ancient warning tablets scattered across the seven nations.

The Unified Ancient Language and Celestia's Runes

The oldest script found on Teyvat is the 'Ancient Unified Script,' carved onto the crumbling stone arches of Enkanomiya, Sal Vindagnyr, The Chasm, and the domains of Celestia. Decoded as a direct substitution cipher for the Latin alphabet, these inscriptions frequently spell out ominous Latin phrases regarding the cyclic nature of time, warnings against opening the abyss, and laments for fallen kings.

This script's presence across vastly distant regions—from the frozen summit of Dragonspine to the deepest underwater trenches of Inazuma—proves that a single, unified human civilization once covered the entire planet before Celestia's cataclysms fractured humanity into separate regional nations.

Regional Alphabets and Cultural Modernization

In the modern era, each nation developed its own distinctive writing system adapted to its cultural identity: • Mondstadt Script: A flowing, gothic-influenced Latin cipher seen on tavern chalkboards, library books, and Knights of Favonius notices. • Liyue Script: A sophisticated seal script adapted from ancient Chinese clerical and bronze characters, visible on merchant contracts, the banners of Feiyun Commerce Guild, and the walls of Wangsheng Funeral Parlor. • Inazuma Script: A stylized calligraphic syllabary derived from Japanese kana, appearing on torii gate inscriptions, light novel covers, and festival lanterns. • Sumeru Script: A geometric, flowing script reminiscent of ancient Middle Eastern and Sanskrit calligraphy, engraved on the walls of the House of Daena and the Akasha consoles. • Fontaine Script: An elegant, serif-heavy art-deco typeface adorning opera playbills, legal codes, and clockwork Meka serial numbers.

The Abyssal and Hilichurlian Tongues

Outside human civilization exist two critical non-human writing systems: the Abyssal Script and Hilichurlian. The Abyssal Script, found on the robes of Abyss Lectors and Khaenri'ahn ruin machinery, uses inverted, angular runes that channel primeval dark energy. When deciphered, these runes often contain mathematical formulas, void curses, or Khaenri'ahn serial designations.

Meanwhile, the spoken and written Hilichurlian tongue—extensively studied in-game by the scholar Ella Musk—represents the degraded remnant of Khaenri'ah's common speech. Phrases like 'Olah!' (greeting) and 'Celi dada, mimi nunu!' (pleasant campfire song) show that even beneath their beastly curse, Hilichurls preserve a fragile, poignant spark of linguistic memory from their lost human lives.
