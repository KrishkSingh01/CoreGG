01 • Player fights

Win more Bedrock fights with a plan

Good aim and a calm hotbar matter more than rushing every hit.

Before the fight

Set up your controls and items

  • Use a practice world or the same server where you plan to fight. Server settings can change damage, gear, and allowed items.
  • Put your sword, blocks, food, bow, and healing items in the same slots every time.
  • Pick a look sensitivity you can control. Practise keeping your crosshair on a moving target.
  • Check your health and food before you start. Keep a way out, such as blocks or a safe route.
Aim

Track the player, not the air

Follow the opponent's chest with your crosshair. Make small corrections as they move instead of swinging your view wildly.

Use a bow when you have space. Aim a little ahead of a player who is running.

Movement

Move sideways while you attack

Strafe left and right so you are harder to hit. Change direction sometimes; repeating one pattern makes you easier to predict.

Use blocks or corners to break line of sight when you need to heal or reset.

Review

Find one mistake to fix

After each fight, ask whether aim, distance, food, gear, or your route caused the loss. Practise that one thing before changing your whole setup.

Edition tip: Some fight advice relies on Java-only timing or server plug-ins. Test your weapon rhythm in Bedrock and follow the rules of the server you join.
02 • Fall saves

Practise the MLG water-bucket clutch

MLG is a last-second save from a fall. Start low, then practise from higher places.

Four steps

Make the landing easier

  1. Use a test world with fall damage on and a large, flat landing area.
  2. Put a water bucket in a fixed hotbar slot. Look at the block where you expect to land.
  3. Place the water just before you hit the ground. In Bedrock, the exact input depends on your device, so practise the controls you use.
  4. Pick up the water and repeat. Practise while moving only after you can land safely.
Remember: Water cannot be placed in the Nether. A clutch can fail if you aim too late, miss the block, or have lag. Carry another safe way down when the items matter.
03 • Movement practice

Learn speed bridging one step at a time

A steady bridge is useful. Add speed only when you can place blocks without gaps.

1 · Safe start

Use sneak near the edge

Stand at the edge, sneak, aim at the last block, and place one block at a time. Sneaking helps stop you walking off ledges.

2 · Build a rhythm

Practise on a wide platform

In Creative, move back a little, aim at the next placement spot, and place a block. Repeat the same motion until the bridge is straight.

3 · Speed up

Make small changes

Increase your pace a little at a time. If blocks start missing, slow down and adjust your aim, movement, or button layout.

Device tip: Keyboard, controller, and touch controls feel different. Change your controls in Settings if needed, then practise with the same setup you use in matches.
04 • Redstone defense

Build a simple trap or warning

Try harmless signals first. Use player-damaging traps only where everyone agrees they are allowed.

Tripwire alert

Make a lamp turn on

  1. Place two tripwire hooks facing each other across a narrow path.
  2. Connect the hooks with string. A player crossing the string sends a redstone signal.
  3. Connect the signal to a lamp or a door using redstone dust.
  4. Walk through it from both sides to test the whole path.
Chest alert

Use a trapped chest

A trapped chest gives a redstone signal when someone opens it. Connect it to a lamp, bell, or door, then test it with a friend before hiding the wiring.

  • Leave yourself a safe exit.
  • Do not build traps in public areas or another player's base unless the rules allow it.
  • Keep the trap simple enough that you can reset and repair it.
Spot a trap: Look for string across paths, odd floor blocks, and redstone near a chest. Check corners and ceilings before stepping into a strange room.
05 • Farms and redstone

Test farms in the Bedrock version you play

A build copied from Java may not work the same way in Bedrock.

Copy first

Protect your main world

Make a copy before changing a large farm or redstone machine. Test the copy in Creative so a mistake does not damage your base.

Measure output

Count items over time

Run the farm for ten minutes with the same settings. Count the items, note where you stood, and write down the game version.

Change one thing

Find what affects the farm

Check the build, nearby caves, player distance, and world settings. Change one part at a time so you know what helped.

Version note: Farm output can change with game updates and world settings. Keep the version and settings with your notes, and check the official release notes after updates.
06 • Worlds and multiplayer

Protect your world before adding content

Add-ons can change blocks, items, mobs, and recipes. Check what a pack does before using it in a long-running world.

Add-ons

Use the pack settings

Bedrock Add-Ons can include resource packs and behavior packs. From the world settings, check the available packs and activate the ones you want. For a Realm, change the world slot settings and wait for the upload to finish.

Try new packs in a copy first. Two add-ons can conflict or change the same parts of a world.

Official steps for activating Bedrock Add-Ons

Backups

Keep a clean restore point

Before an update, add-on, or large build, download or copy the world. If something breaks, use the clean copy instead of experimenting on your only save.

  • Keep one untouched copy somewhere safe.
  • Name test copies with the date and what you changed.
  • Ask the Realm owner before changing shared settings.
07 • Practice plan

A short routine for steady improvement

Practise one skill at a time so you can see what is getting better.

5 minutes

Track a moving target

Use a safe target, follow it with your crosshair, and practise moving sideways without losing aim.

5 minutes

Place bridge blocks

Build a short bridge with sneak on. Repeat until you can place each block without looking down for long.

5 minutes

Test one machine

Build a tiny redstone circuit, test each part, and write down what happened in your Bedrock version.

Quick words

MLG: a last-second move that prevents fall damage.

Strafe: move sideways while facing a target.

Hotbar: the row of items at the bottom of your screen.

Redstone signal: power that activates a lamp, door, or machine.

Latency: delay between your action and what the server shows.

World copy: a separate save used to test changes safely.
